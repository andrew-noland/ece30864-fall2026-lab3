Q1.

Pros:
1. Agile allows teams to adapt quickly when requirements or customer needs change during development.
2. Agile provides frequent feedback through short development cycles, allowing problems to be found and corrected earlier

Cons:
1. Since requirements can frequently change, it can be harder to predict the exact schedule, cost, and final scope of a project.
2. Agile provides frequent communication and participation from team members and stakeholders, which can requie significant time and coordination


Q2.

Scrum Master:
The scrum master helps the team follow scrumpractices and removes obstacles that prevent the development team from completing its work.
The scrum master also facilitates scrum meetings and helps improve communication and teamwork.

Product Owner:
The product owner represents the needs of the customer and stakeholders.
The product owner manages and prioritizes the product backlog and decides which features or requirements provide the most value to the product.


Q3.

1. Sprint Planning:
    - The team determines which backlog items will be completed during the sprint
    - The team dicusses how the selected work will be completed and establishes the sprint goal

2. Daily Scrum:
    - Team Members discuss their progress and what work they plan to complete next.
    - The team identifies problems or blockers that may prevent progress

3. Sprint Review:
    - The completed work from the sprint is reviewed and feedback is gathered.
    - The team discusses what went well, what could be improved, and changes that should be made during the next sprint.


Q4.

Epic:
A large body of work that normally contains multiple related stories or tasks and may require multiple development cycles to complete.

Story:
Afeature or requirement usually written from the user's prespectuive that describes functionality that provides value to the user.

Bug:
An issue describing something in the software that does not behave as intended and needs to be corrected

Task:
A specific piece of work that needs to be completed but is not necessarily a user-facing feature or software defect.
